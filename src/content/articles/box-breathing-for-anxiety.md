Racing heart, tight chest, thoughts that won't slow down — anxiety often shows up in the body before you can name what triggered it. Box breathing is one of the fastest ways to talk your nervous system back down, and it doesn't require anything except your own breath and a few minutes of attention.

## What is box breathing?

Box breathing (also called four-square breathing) is a simple pattern: inhale, hold, exhale, hold, each for the same count. Picture tracing the four sides of a square as you go:

1. **Inhale** through your nose for 4 counts
2. **Hold** the breath for 4 counts
3. **Exhale** through your mouth for 4 counts
4. **Hold** at the bottom for 4 counts

Repeat the cycle for four to eight rounds. The equal counts are the point — a steady, predictable rhythm gives your body a clear signal that it's safe to slow down.

## Why it works

When anxiety spikes, breathing tends to get short and shallow, which keeps your sympathetic nervous system — the "fight or flight" branch — switched on. Slowing your exhale and adding a deliberate pause activates the opposite system, the parasympathetic response, which is responsible for rest and recovery. You're not fighting the anxious feeling directly; you're changing the physiological conditions that are feeding it.

It also gives your mind a concrete, boring task. Counting to four, four times in a row, leaves little room for the thought spiral to keep running unattended.

## How to practice it

- Find a position where your spine is reasonably upright — sitting is fine, you don't need to lie down.
- Start with a slower count if 4 seconds feels rushed. Consistency matters more than speed.
- Keep your shoulders relaxed and let the breath expand your belly, not just your chest.
- If holding your breath for 4 counts feels uncomfortable at first, shorten it to 2 and build up over a few sessions.
- Use it proactively, not just in a crisis — a few rounds before a stressful meeting or at the start of the day trains the response so it's easier to access when you actually need it.

## A note on limits

Box breathing is a self-regulation tool, not medical treatment. It can take the edge off anxious moments and, with regular practice, may lower your overall baseline stress — but it isn't a substitute for therapy or medical care. If anxiety is frequent, intense, or getting in the way of daily life, it's worth talking to a doctor or therapist. If you're in crisis, please reach out to a mental health professional or local emergency services right away.
