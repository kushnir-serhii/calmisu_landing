Panic can make the present moment feel like it's slipping away — your mind jumps to worst-case scenarios while your body reacts as if they're already happening. Grounding techniques work by deliberately redirecting your attention to what's real and physically here, right now. They won't erase the panic instantly, but they give it somewhere to land other than the spiral.

## 1. The 5-4-3-2-1 method

Name, out loud or in your head:

- **5** things you can see
- **4** things you can touch
- **3** things you can hear
- **2** things you can smell
- **1** thing you can taste

Go slowly and be specific — not just "a chair," but its color, texture, the way light hits it. The specificity is what pulls your attention out of the anxious loop and into your senses.

## 2. Cold water or temperature shift

Splash cold water on your face, hold an ice cube, or step outside into cooler air. A sudden change in temperature triggers a physical reset that can interrupt a panic response faster than thinking your way out of it.

## 3. Feet on the floor

Press both feet flat into the ground and notice the contact — the texture under your shoes, the support beneath you. This is a quiet, discreet technique you can use anywhere, including in a meeting or on public transport, without anyone noticing.

## 4. Name the room

Describe your physical surroundings in plain, factual language: "I'm sitting in a blue chair. The window is to my left. It's Tuesday afternoon." Facts, not judgments. This gives your mind a low-stakes task and reinforces that you are safe in this specific place, right now.

## 5. Slow, counted exhale

Grounding pairs well with breath work. A slow exhale — longer than your inhale — nudges your body out of fight-or-flight. Try inhaling for 4 counts and exhaling for 6, a few times in a row. Calmisu's guided breathing flow uses a similar paced pattern if you'd rather follow along than count yourself.

## When to use these

Grounding techniques work best when practiced before you're in crisis, so the steps feel familiar under pressure. Try running through one during a calm moment so it's not the first time you're using it when panic actually hits.

## A note on limits

These techniques can help you get through an acute moment of panic, but they aren't a diagnosis or a treatment for panic disorder. If panic attacks are frequent, severe, or you're avoiding parts of your life because of them, please talk to a doctor or therapist — that's the kind of support grounding exercises can't replace. If you're in immediate danger or crisis, contact a mental health professional or local emergency services right away.
